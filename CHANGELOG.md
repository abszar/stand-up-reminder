# Changelog

All notable changes to Stand Up Reminder are documented in this file.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **An edge glow for the standing check.** When the pill asks whether you are
  still up, coral now bleeds in from all four edges of every monitor and
  fades away again over three seconds — most of that at full strength, since
  the point is to be seen by someone who was looking elsewhere when it began. The pill sits where you are not looking;
  this puts the same question in peripheral vision wherever you are looking.
  It covers nothing — the strips are transparent gradients that take no focus
  and pass every click straight through to whatever is underneath — and it is
  held back in Discreet mode along with everything else. It is the one thing
  in the application that eases rather than cutting between frames, because
  at that size a hard on-off reads as a fault rather than a signal.
- **Discreet mode**, a switch in the top-bar menu for the minutes somebody
  else is watching your screen. The break moves out of the middle to a card
  in the bottom right corner carrying the count and three buttons and nothing
  else, the screen is no longer dimmed, eye cards are held back, and the
  application goes silent. It changes no setting — it is a lid over them, and
  everything comes back the moment it comes off. It is deliberately not saved
  across a restart: it is switched on for a meeting, and being silently muted
  for a week is the worse bug.
- **Three new sounds.** The break now has a call of its own — a repeated note
  then a jump, the only cue in the set that repeats a note — and a soft
  falling pair when its two minutes are up. Keeping a break earns a fanfare:
  five notes climbing an octave and a half, fired both when you say you are
  back and when you close the standing pill, because from your side those are
  the same event.
- The break's sounds now ship as files rather than asking the desktop for a
  theme sound, so they play whether or not GSound is installed.
- **Eye breaks.** A small card arrives every twenty minutes asking for twenty
  seconds — the twenty-twenty-twenty rule — and closes itself. It dims
  nothing, takes no focus and never lands on top of a break card, because a
  prompt that arrives three times an hour only works if ignoring one costs
  nothing. Three prompts share the rotation: **Look far**, drawn as a window
  with drifting cloud, a flickering sun and hills hazed into the distance;
  **Close your eyes**, which runs three firm squeezes before a long rest and
  darkens the card to the void while your eyes are shut; and **Move your
  eyes**, which walks a lit dot around a ring with the eyes at its centre.
  Looking into the distance has the clearest evidence behind it, so it takes
  four turns of the six.
- Five chiptune cues for the eye breaks, shipped as WAV files generated by
  `scripts/make-sounds.py`: two or three notes, one waveform, a hard four
  millisecond envelope. Rising means do something, falling means settle, so
  the prompts can be told apart without looking at the screen.
- Sounds now play without GSound. Where the library is missing — it is an
  optional dependency and absent on plenty of desktops — the cues that ship
  as files fall back to `paplay` or `aplay`, so the application's own sounds
  work everywhere.

### Fixed

- Saying *I'm back* from the menu now closes whichever surface is carrying the
  break rather than only the centred card, so it works in Discreet mode too,
  and sounds the same fanfare the break card's own button does.
- **The standing pill's "still there?" check was invisible.** It lit the ring
  for 120 milliseconds once every ten minutes — but forgetting to close the
  pill means you were not looking at it, so a flash that short could never
  land. The check is a state now: the whole pill floods with colour for 2.6
  seconds, then holds it until answered, and floods again on the same ten
  minutes if it goes unanswered. Twenty minutes unanswered turns it coral.
  Clicking the pill body says you are still up; dragging still moves it, the
  two told apart by whether the pointer moved. It never lapses back to calm
  on its own, because an unanswered pill holds the work interval open and no
  break falls due at all while it does.
- Discreet mode no longer lets the pre-break warning through. The break card
  was routed to the corner but the fifteen-second warning that precedes it
  was not, so the centred card still appeared before every break.
- **The eye-break interval ran four times too fast.** The clock counted ticks
  and treated each as a second, but the tick is four times a second, so a
  twenty-minute setting produced a card every five minutes. It is measured
  against the clock now, like the card's own countdown.
- The eye-break rotation no longer restarts at *Look far* every time the
  application does. Its position was held in memory only, so a machine
  restarted daily would see the first prompt of the rotation and little else.
  It is now saved with the rest of the settings.

### Changed

- **Every settings row now carries an icon**, drawn as pixels in code rather
  than shipped as art: a clock for the work interval, a cup for the break, a
  cursor for active-only counting, a door for leaving the desk, a window and
  a shut eye and a pair of arrows for the three eye prompts, and so on. The
  five headings are coloured — amber, mint, sky, plum, coral — and a row's own
  icon stays grey until the row is switched on, then takes its heading's
  colour. Colour says what is on; shape says what it is.
- **The top-bar menu carries the two actions worth reaching without opening a
  window:** *I'm back — restart the timer*, which greys out when there is no
  break to come back from, and *Statistics*. The control window keeps both.
- The list of sounds in the settings page folds away behind one line. It has
  a row per declared cue and grows every time a sound is added, which is a
  lot of panel for a list you set once.
- **Sound is now a master switch over a list.** The settings page has a SOUND
  section: one switch that silences the whole application, and beneath it one
  row per sound, so a cue can be dropped without losing the rest. Sounds are
  played by declaring a cue rather than naming a system event at the call
  site, which means a sound added in a later version passes both switches and
  arrives with its own settings row without anyone wiring it up. The choice is
  stored as the set of *muted* cues, so a new cue is audible by default and
  settings files written before it existed need no migration.
- Removed a duplicated `Gtk.CssProvider()` construction in application startup.
- Settings rows no longer break a two-word label in half: a wrapping label
  asks for the width of its longest word and no more, so the rows now carry a
  minimum width. The panel also scrolls, having grown longer than some screens.

- **Standing holds the work interval, and sitting down starts it afresh.**
  While the standing pill is counting, no break falls due and no warning
  fires — time on your feet is the break, so reminders no longer stack up
  behind it. Closing the pill restarts the full work interval from the
  moment you sit, which is what the research on uninterrupted sitting
  actually measures: a long stand is neither punished with a short interval
  nor banked for a longer one.
- The pill's stop control is now a coral cross at its foot, so closing the
  count reads as closing the count.
- **The pill's ring now speaks.** One amber blink every ten minutes asks
  whether you are still up, so a pill left running after you sat down gets
  noticed rather than silently holding the timer all afternoon. Three mint
  blinks every half hour ask for a set of squats or calf raises — the part
  standing on its own does not buy you. Neither opens anything nor needs
  dismissing, and under reduced motion each is held as a still colour.

- **The whole interface is now an 8-bit pixel HUD**, built to a design
  specification: an eleven-colour palette, the Silkscreen bitmap face (SIL
  Open Font License, shipped with the application), sprite display digits,
  and window frames of two hard rings with cut corners. The break card, the
  standing pill, the score window, the dimmer and the top-bar icon are all
  redrawn on a four-pixel art grid.
- Motion throughout, on fixed frame clocks and never eased: the card wipes
  open in six bands, the colon keeps a one-second heartbeat, the countdown
  reddens and shudders in its last ten seconds, the progress bar dies one
  cell at a time and flips coral from the left when the end is near, a mint
  burst marks a kept break, a coral ring and a shake mark one that was not,
  the pill slides in and its face bobs, and the score screen counts up and
  paints its grid in column by column. Everything reads
  `org.gnome.desktop.interface enable-animations` and falls back to a still.
- The standing pill is vertical, docked flush to the right edge, with the
  time stacked as minutes over seconds and an hours row that opens past the
  hour.
- The top-bar menu is down to what the shell can draw honestly — the status
  line, the day's blocks, and **Open Stand Up Reminder**. Every action moved
  into a new pixel **control window**: start a break, restart the timer,
  stand up or sit down, pause for a while or until you resume, and the score,
  settings and quit. GNOME Shell renders that menu from labels sent over DBus,
  so the application cannot give those rows the interface face; the window it
  draws itself can.
- A **standing counter you can start yourself**, from the control window or
  the break card, for the times you stand up without waiting to be asked. A
  break answered while you are already up keeps the count running rather than
  restarting it.
- The statistics popup is now the **Score** window, and the durations,
  timing and options submenus have moved into a **Settings** panel, leaving
  the top-bar menu seven flat rows. The menu's day row spells today out in
  block glyphs.
- Copy throughout is shorter and in the first person: *Up you get*, *Nice
  one*, *5 more min*, *Skip it*, *Didn't take it*, *Still standing*.

### Added

- **Standing mode** for standing desks: the break window offers *I'm
  standing — keep counting* (shortcut `T`) while the break runs and once it
  is over. It counts the break as taken, starts a fresh work interval, and
  opens a small pill on the right edge of the screen counting how long you
  have been up. The pill is dragged up and down the edge, remembers where it
  was left, and is closed with its ✕ when you sit back down; the work
  countdown that started when you stood up simply carries on.

- A day timeline on the break window and the statistics popup: each
  recorded outcome is stored with its time and drawn as a colored dot on a
  track spanning the worked part of the day (green taken, gray-teal away,
  red missed, orange skipped, pale snoozed). The statistics popup adds the
  track's start and end times and a color legend. The whole timeline block
  stays hidden until the day has its first recorded outcome.
- The statistics popup carries the mood emoji beside its score, labels that
  score as the week's, and adds today's score on its own line.
- A contribution-style grid of the last twelve weeks in the statistics
  popup, a column per week and a row per weekday, each day shaded by how
  well it was kept and captioned on hover with its counts and score. It
  replaces the row of weekday counts.

### Changed

- The pre-break countdown offers the same snooze and skip actions as the
  break itself, with the same `S` and `K` shortcuts, so a break can be put
  off before it interrupts anything. Snoozing from the warning delays the
  break; skipping starts a fresh work interval.
- The pre-break countdown starts white and reddens as the break approaches.
- History keeps twelve weeks of daily counters instead of thirty days, and
  individual break times only for the last week.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-07-25

### Added

- The break window shows the day's and the week's adherence scores with a
  mood emoji, e.g. "Today: 75% 🙂 · This week: 92% 😄".
- A **Statistics** entry in the indicator menu that opens a popup styled
  like the break card, showing today's counters, the last seven days with a
  per-day row of breaks taken, and a weekly adherence score (breaks taken
  against missed and skipped) with a one-line verdict.
- An **I didn't take this break** button beside the return confirmation, for
  breaks that finished while working elsewhere. It restarts the work timer
  and counts the break as missed, a new category in the daily summary.

### Changed

- The pre-break desktop notification is replaced by the break window itself,
  which now opens 15 seconds before the break with a live countdown and the
  message "Time to stand up in 0:15".

- Time away from the keyboard now counts as a break only after a configurable
  threshold (10 minutes by default, chosen from the Options menu) instead of
  after a single break length. Short pauses — reading, a phone call, working
  on another computer — no longer silently restart the work timer.
- Breaks credited for time away are counted separately from breaks taken, so
  the daily summary reflects only the breaks that were really taken.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-07-21

### Added

- Configurable work interval and break length, chosen from the indicator menu
  and stored with the rest of the settings.
- Countdown to the next break beside the top-bar icon, which can be hidden.
- Desktop notification shortly before a break begins.
- Pausing for 30 minutes, an hour, or until reminders are resumed.
- Idle detection that counts a long stretch away from the keyboard as a break
  already taken.
- Daily counts of breaks taken, skipped, and snoozed, shown in the menu and
  kept for 30 days.
- Keyboard shortcuts in the break window: `S` to snooze, `K` to skip, and
  `Enter` to confirm a return.
- Optional sound cues at the start and end of a break.
- Dimming of monitors that are not showing the break window.
- French translation and a gettext workflow for adding more languages.
- Debian package build, a locale-pinned test runner, and continuous
  integration covering tests, shell scripts, catalogues, and packaging.

### Changed

- The break window opens fullscreen on Wayland, which does not allow an
  application to place a window or keep it above others.
- The launcher is installed as `io.github.abdelali.StandUpReminder.desktop`
  so that GNOME attributes break notifications to the application. Installing
  removes the previous launcher name.
- Settings are read field by field, so one unusable value no longer discards
  the rest of the configuration.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-07-20

### Added

- Native Ubuntu GNOME top-bar indicator and centered standing-break window.
- Deterministic 30-minute work and two-minute break cycle.
- Repeatable five-minute snoozing and full-break skipping.
- Explicit return confirmation after a completed break.
- Active-time and wall-clock timing modes for lock and suspend behavior.
- Graphical-login startup, Applications menu launcher, and clean Quit flow.
- User-local install, update, and uninstall scripts.
- Automated scheduler, presentation, settings, and installation tests.
